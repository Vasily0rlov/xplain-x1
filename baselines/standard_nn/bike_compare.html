On the programme's own metric — fidelity (held-out R² in log1p space, the scale both nets train in) — they are close: 0.9424 (standard) vs 0.8909 (x1), both near the 0.9476 reference ceiling. In raw count space the bigger dense net fits the heavy-tailed peak hours harder (R² 0.914 vs 0.6934, RMSE 54.78 vs 103.45) — a real cost of the minimal model's small capacity, honestly shown, not hidden. The decisive difference is legibility, measured by the same instrument: the standard net spreads the signal across 32 dense units (median fan-in 10.0, 0 legible), while XPLAIN-x1 carries it in 6 sparse units (median fan-in 4.0, 3 legible) whose supports you can read off the right-hand DAG — hour, temperature, year. Only the x1 model comes with a certifiable function decomposition on top.
